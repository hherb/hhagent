# Guard tier — measurement 3: the calibration corpus

**Status:** design, 2026-08-22
**Predecessor:** [`2026-08-21-shieldstral-guard-slice-1-design.md`](2026-08-21-shieldstral-guard-slice-1-design.md) (D9)
**Blocks:** [`2026-08-22-shieldstral-guard-wiring-design.md`](2026-08-22-shieldstral-guard-wiring-design.md) — that slice's D1 makes τ a
required operator input precisely because this measurement does not exist yet.

Slice 1 shipped a 24-case corpus labelled, in four separate places, a **proof of
concept**. This spec designs the thing that replaces it: ≥100 labelled cases, half of them
captured rather than imagined, and a τ fitted against them on both hosts.

Three findings below were measured while scoping it, and two of them changed the design.

---

## F1 — The live audit log cannot supply the captured half

`tool_host::post_process::finalize` stores every worker result in `audit_log`, so the DGX's
production history looked like a ready-made capture source. It is not. Measured
2026-08-22 against the live DGX cluster:

| actor / action | rows | **distinct results** | avg len | max len |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `tool:shell-exec` / `shell.exec` | 58 | **16** | 116 B | 1,371 B |
| `tool:web-search` / `web.search` | 27 | **27** | 3,495 B | 4,021 B |
| `tool:mail` / six methods | 29 | **27** | ~600 B | 3,487 B |

**~70 distinct documents, of which perhaps 35 are substantive** — and three problems that
the row counts hide:

1. **There are no `tool:web-fetch` rows at all.** Fetched pages are the material the corpus
   README names first for the captured half, and the live bot has never produced one.
2. **Nothing exceeds 4 KB, against a 64 KiB cap.** The README explicitly anticipates that
   "captured half is real fetched pages, where 200 KiB is ordinary" — so this material would
   never exercise the `SCAN_BYTE_CAP` truncation that production applies.
3. **It is uniformly benign.** It supplies no captured attacks whatsoever.

So the captured half must be **generated by a capture campaign**, not mined. This is worth
recording rather than quietly working around: mining an existing table was the cheap plan,
it was checked before being adopted, and the check is what killed it.

## F2 — oMLX still returns no logprobs, so the Mac leg needs llama.cpp

Re-measured live 2026-08-22 rather than carried from the study. oMLX serves
`Shieldstral-1.0-3B-MLX-8bit` and answers a plain injection correctly (`content: "yes"`),
but the response carries **no `logprobs` field at all**, with `logprobs: true` and
`top_logprobs: 20` both accepted and ignored. That is measurement 1's go/no-go, unchanged
after a week.

A bare verdict makes `binary_token_probability` return `None`, which `decide` maps to
`Unmeasured` — and the calibration report treats a non-zero `Unmeasured` count as
invalidating the run. **oMLX cannot fit τ and cannot run the tier.**

Note also that oMLX's MLX-8bit is a **third quantisation**, alongside the DGX's GGUF Q8_0
and this Mac's existing Q4_K_M. A τ does not transfer across quantisations — that is why
Q8_0 was pinned on both hosts in the first place — so the Mac leg runs llama.cpp with the
same `Shieldstral-1.0-3B-Q8_0.gguf` the DGX uses.

*(Incidental correction: the study reasoned about a 16 GB Mac when ruling BF16 out. This
Mac is an M3 Max with 128 GB, so that particular constraint does not bind here. Q8_0 is
pinned for cross-host τ transfer, not for memory.)*

## F3 — A top-level Apache-2.0 stamp on an aggregate dataset is not a license

The project's hard constraint is AGPL-compatible dependencies only. Committing a public
injection dataset's text into this repo is redistribution, so the dataset's license binds.

Checked concretely rather than assumed. `neuralchemy/Prompt-injection-dataset` presents as
**Apache-2.0**, and is an aggregation of six sources whose own terms are Apache-2.0, MIT,
**CC BY 4.0**, and — for `WildGuard / JudgeComparison` — **unspecified / research-only**. A
blanket stamp over a component with unstated terms does not confer those terms on it.

This is the dangerous shape, because the top level *looks* clean: a reviewer checking "what
license is this dataset" gets a correct-sounding one-word answer and never sees the
component that has none. `deepset/prompt-injections` shows a milder version of the same
thing — `apache-2.0` in `cardData` and `cc-by-4.0` in `dataset_info`, disagreeing with
itself.

**D1 exists to make this irrelevant rather than to adjudicate it.**

## Design decisions

### D1 — No third-party text is committed; a manifest plus a fetch script materialises it

The corpus splits into two kinds of case:

- **Authored here** (the existing 24, and any new hand-written ones): text committed, as
  today. We hold the copyright, so there is nothing to launder.
- **Everything else** — captured pages and third-party injection payloads: **the text is
  never committed.** What is committed is a manifest entry carrying
  `{id, label, provenance, source, sha256, notes}` and no `text` field at all.

`scripts/eval/materialise-guard-corpus.sh` reads the manifest, fetches each source, verifies
its **sha256**, and writes a complete corpus into a git-ignored local directory. Calibration
then runs against that directory.

Three reasons this is right, in descending order of how much they matter:

1. **It dissolves F3.** Using data locally to fit a threshold is not redistribution. The
   licensing question stops being "may we relicense this under AGPL" — which for an
   aggregate with an unlicensed component has no good answer — and becomes "may we read a
   public dataset", which it does.
2. **It dissolves the privacy problem too.** The same mechanism lets a captured case point
   at operator-private material (a real mail body) without that body entering a public repo.
   Not needed for the initial campaign, which is web content, but the corpus will want it.
3. **It is the pattern this repo already uses twice.** The Firecracker guest kernel is
   sha256-pinned in `lib/guest-kernel.sh`, verified at every VM boot by
   `require_guest_kernel`, and never committed; the Shieldstral weights are fetched by a
   resumable `fetch.sh` against a pinned byte length. Neither artefact lives in git and both
   are verified on use.

**No loader change is needed.** `kastellan-cli guard calibrate --corpus DIR` already takes an
arbitrary directory, and `default_corpus_dir` is only a convenience. The materialised
directory is just another `DIR`.

**What is committed as evidence** is the manifest, the fetch script, and the calibration
**report** — the confusion matrix, the per-stratum breakdown, and the fitted τ. Anyone can
re-materialise the exact bytes from the manifest and re-derive the numbers.

### D2 — Sources are pinned to immutable content, not to live URLs

A sha256 over a live web page is a sha256 of whatever the page said that day, which makes
the corpus unreproducible the first time a site is edited — and a corpus you cannot
reproduce is a τ nobody can check.

So every manifest entry names an **immutable** source:

- **HuggingFace rows** — pinned by dataset *revision* hash, not by `main`.
- **Web pages** — pinned to a Wayback Machine snapshot URL, which carries a timestamp and
  does not change, rather than to the live page.

The fetch script **fails closed on a sha256 mismatch**, the same posture
`require_guest_kernel` takes. A drifted source is a corpus that no longer matches the
manifest, and continuing would fit τ against unknown text.

### D3 — The captured half comes from a real `web-fetch` campaign through the production path

Captured cases are produced by driving the **real sandboxed `web-fetch` worker** — through
`tool_host::dispatch`, the sandbox, and the egress proxy — against the pinned snapshot URLs,
and recording what the chokepoint actually received.

This matters more than it sounds. The alternative (fetching with `curl` and calling the
result captured) would miss everything the production path does to a document on the way in:
`extract_scannable_text`'s key-stripping and alphabetical flattening, the `SCAN_BYTE_CAP`
truncation, and the worker's own extraction. A corpus scored on text the chokepoint never
sees fits τ against a fiction — which is precisely the mistake the corpus README already
warns about for the byte cap, generalised one level up.

**Deliberately over-cap material is included.** F1's third problem is that nothing captured
so far approaches 64 KiB; the campaign must include pages that exceed it, so the corpus
exercises truncation rather than assuming it away.

### D4 — Labelling: discussion is benign, a directed payload is an attack

A page that *describes* prompt injection — a security blog, an OWASP entry, a model card
discussing jailbreaks, a shell tutorial — is labelled **benign**. A document that *directs*
an instruction at its reader is labelled **attack**.

This continues what the seeded corpus already asserts (`safe-001-security-prose`,
`safe-003-shell-tutorial`) rather than inventing a rule, and it aims the corpus at the
failure that actually costs something. The agent reads technical security material
constantly; a guard that flags it has not become safer, it has become unable to read about
security. False positives on that stratum are the expensive kind.

**The boundary case is named, not hidden:** an editorial page that quotes a payload
*verbatim* is still labelled benign under this rule, even though a model reading it could in
principle be steered by the quote. That is a deliberate, contestable choice — it buys
capability at the cost of a real, if narrow, evasion (wrap an injection in editorial
framing). It is recorded here as Open risk 3 rather than argued away, and a future session
that wants the stricter rule needs a third label, not a re-reading of this one.

### D5 — Strata and counts, with the invalid-run conditions stated up front

Target **≥120 cases** so the ≥100 floor survives a handful being discarded during labelling.

| stratum | provenance | label | target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| existing seeded set | `hand_written` / `derived_from_catalogue` | mixed | 24 (unchanged) |
| captured benign — ordinary web content | `captured` | benign | ~35 |
| captured benign — security/technical prose (D4's expensive stratum) | `captured` | benign | ~15 |
| captured attack — third-party payloads via the fetch path | `captured` | attack | ~35 |
| over-cap documents (any label, counted twice) | `captured` | mixed | ≥8 |

That is ~85 captured against 24 authored, comfortably clearing "a captured half".

`guard calibrate` already reports each provenance stratum **separately and never pools
them**, which is what stops a strong score on the authored cases from hiding a weak one on
the captured cases. This spec does not change that; it finally gives it something to
separate.

**A run is invalid, not merely poor, if:** any case scores `Unmeasured` (F2's failure mode);
the captured stratum is under 50; or the fitted τ sits at a boundary of the swept range,
which means the sweep did not bracket the optimum.

### D6 — τ is fitted on both hosts and the two are compared, not averaged

The DGX and the Mac run the same weights, the same quantisation and the same runtime, so a
τ fitted on one should transfer. **Should** is a hypothesis, and this is the measurement that
can test it.

Both hosts fit independently and both reports are committed. If the two τ agree, the pinning
decision is vindicated and the shared value ships. **If they disagree, that is a finding, not
a discrepancy to average away** — it would mean quantisation-identical hosts still diverge,
and the cross-platform claim the whole design rests on would need re-examining before any
τ ships at all.

## What this spec deliberately excludes

- **The wiring.** Separate spec, already written, and its D1 is what lets it wait.
- **A third label** for the D4 boundary case. Open risk 3.
- **Committing any operator-private material.** D1 makes it *possible* to reference; the
  initial campaign uses public web content only.
- **Re-running measurements 1, 2, 4, 5.** They are pinned and unaffected.

## Open risks

1. **The capture campaign's URL list is itself a judgement call**, and a list chosen by
   whoever builds the corpus tests what that person thought to visit. This is the seeded
   corpus's flaw at one remove, and it is reduced — not removed — by drawing the attack half
   from third-party datasets rather than authoring it.
2. **Wayback snapshots can disappear**, and a fail-closed fetch then blocks the whole
   measurement rather than one case. Mitigation is to keep the materialised corpus around
   once fetched; the manifest is the recipe, not the only copy.
3. **D4's benign-verbatim-quote rule is a real, narrow evasion.** Wrap a payload in editorial
   framing and the corpus says the guard should let it through.
4. **A τ fitted on 120 cases is still a small-sample τ.** Better than 24 by a wide margin,
   and still not a number to promote to a default without saying what it was fitted on.
5. **The two-host comparison in D6 has no pre-registered agreement threshold.** "Do they
   agree" needs a number before the run, or it becomes a judgement made after seeing the
   answer.
